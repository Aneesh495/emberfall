# Convex contacts and angular bodies

Bodies may use a closed triangle hull of up to 64 vertices and 124 faces. The loader validates index bounds, oriented edge incidence and convex face planes before accepting the hull. Box and hull pairs use face normals and edge cross products as separating axes. Clipped boundary segments provide one contact witness within the overlap. Round pairs use finite capsule segments. A capsule against a hull uses closest points on the triangle boundary or the least separating face when its centerline intersects the hull.

Angular response is opt-in. A box or sphere has analytic principal moments for uniform density. A capsule uses the moments of its bounding box as an approximation. A dynamic convex hull needs explicitly supplied positive principal inverse inertias. Its local origin represents the center of mass. Contact forces act at the witness, with a two-axis tangent effective mass and a Coulomb friction disk. The solver projects warm-start friction onto the new contact plane and discards angular warm starts when the witness moves more than 75 mm.

Rotation uses a world-space angular momentum step. It estimates the midpoint orientation and maps the same momentum through the midpoint and final inverse inertia tensors. A kinematic body's prescribed angular velocity remains authoritative. Tests check an analytical off-center impulse, torque-free momentum and energy bounds, rotated convex contacts, and rolling generated by contact friction. [Physics tests](../tests/convex_dynamics.cpp)

The translating gameplay and benchmark path keeps its existing fast contacts. Position correction separates penetration along the normal without an angular pose correction. A single witness cannot represent a full resting contact manifold. Continuous collision is bounded to the existing swept extent against static axis-aligned boxes. It does not sweep rotating polyhedra.

The separating-axis candidates and closest-feature methods follow the geometric descriptions in the references. [David Eberly](https://www.geometrictools.com/Documentation/MethodOfSeparatingAxes.pdf) and [Christer Ericson](https://realtimecollisiondetection.net/books/rtcd/toc/index.html)
